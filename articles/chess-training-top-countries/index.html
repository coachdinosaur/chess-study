How the World's Top Chess Countries Train — and Why They Win

Armenia has three million people and three Olympiad titles. Uzbekistan fielded the youngest team in Chennai in 2022 and went unbeaten for gold. India, a nation with no elite chess tradition a generation ago, now owns both Olympic golds and the world championship. None of this is an accident.

Look closely at the countries that dominate chess and a pattern emerges. Each one found a way to do four things: find talent early, train it systematically, keep it competing, and make the whole enterprise worth someone's while — whether that "someone" is a ministry of sport, a billionaire patron, a school network, or a family that reorganized itself around the game. The systems differ enormously. The constants underneath them do not.

This four-part series examines the training models behind the world's strongest chess nations — what each one actually does, why it works, and what an individual player or coach can steal from them.
