Chess Terms

To study chess, you need to know the names of important board areas. This lesson explains kingside, queenside, whiteside, and blackside using files and ranks.

KingsideIdentify the board area made of the e-, f-, g-, and h-files.
QueensideIdentify the board area made of the a-, b-, c-, and d-files.
WhitesideRecognize the lower half of the board: ranks 1–4.
BlacksideRecognize the upper half of the board: ranks 5–8.
Lesson contents
1. The four terms 2. Kingside and Queenside 3. Whiteside and Blackside 4. Four areas together 5. How to remember 6. Review
1

The Four Terms

Each term describes a part of the chessboard. Look at the file letters and rank numbers carefully.

Kingside Files: efgh
Queenside Files: abcd
Whiteside Ranks: 1234
Blackside Ranks: 5678
2

Kingside and Queenside

The kingside and queenside are based on files. Files are the vertical columns named a through h.

KKingside

The kingside is the part of the board from the e-file to the h-file. It includes files e, f, g, h.

QQueenside

The queenside is the part of the board from the a-file to the d-file. It includes files a, b, c, d.

3

Whiteside and Blackside

The whiteside and blackside are based on ranks. Ranks are the horizontal rows numbered 1 through 8.

WWhiteside / Blackside Split

The whiteside is ranks 1, 2, 3, 4. The blackside is ranks 5, 6, 7, 8.

4

Four Board Areas Together

You can combine the terms. For example, a square can be on the whiteside kingside or on the blackside queenside.

4Combined board areas

The vertical split gives kingside and queenside. The horizontal split gives whiteside and blackside.

5

How to Remember

Use these simple clues when looking at the board.

King clue

The king begins on the e-file. The side starting with the e-file is the kingside: e, f, g, h.

Queen clue

The queen begins on the d-file. The side ending with the d-file is the queenside: a, b, c, d.

White clue

White starts at the bottom of the board. The lower half is the whiteside: ranks 1–4.

Black clue

Black starts at the top of the board. The upper half is the blackside: ranks 5–8.

Teacher note
“Whiteside” and “blackside” are classroom-friendly terms for the lower and upper halves of the board. Many chess books simply say “White’s half” and “Black’s half.”
