Rook vs Bishop, Rook vs Knight & Rook-Pawn Rule

The rook-pawn drawing rule completed, then the wrong and right corners in Rook vs Bishop, and the fundamental defensive principle in Rook vs Knight: keep the king and knight together.

  • Know that occupying the bishop-file squares beside the promotion corner also draws against a rook pawn.
  • Understand the wrong corner concept in Rook vs Bishop.
  • Recognise the right corner as a theoretical draw for the bishop.
  • Follow the rook's plan of progressively restricting the bishop.
  • Know that in Rook vs Knight the king and knight must stay together.
  • Spot when a separated knight becomes the target.
Continuation

Rook-Pawn Drawing Rule

We continue from Position 1.16. Black's only chance is to prevent the white king from getting in front of the pawn.

Position 1.16 · Rook-pawn drawing rule · White to move

FEN: 8/8/8/8/p7/1k6/3K4/8 w - - 0 1 · Key squares: c1, c2

The marked squares c1 and c2 are the bishop-file squares next to the promotion corner a1. White can hold the draw by occupying one of them.

Main line — Black pushes the pawn

1...a4 2.Kb1 a3 3.Ka1

This reaches the well-known drawn rook-pawn position.

Main continuation — White holds

2.Kc2!

White prevents the black king from escaping the corner.

Continuation

2...a4 3.Kc1 a3 4.Kc2 Ka1 5.Kc1 =

Nothing can be done. The black king cannot escape, and pushing the pawn immediately stalemates the king.

Variation

2...Ka3 3.Kb1 =

Even several doubled rook pawns would not change the result.

Key Idea

Against a rook pawn, the defender draws not only by standing directly in front of the pawn, but also by occupying one of the two bishop-file squares next to the promotion corner (in this example, c1 and c2).

Ending 6

Rook vs Bishop: The Wrong Corner

Rook vs Bishop is normally drawn, but becomes dangerous when the defending king is trapped in the wrong corner.

Definition

The wrong corner is the corner having the same colour as the bishop's squares. When the defending king is trapped there, the rook can force the bishop off its safe squares and deliver mate.

Lesson

The Wrong Corner

Black is trapped in the wrong corner. White wins by forcing the bishop out.

Position 1.17 · Rook vs Bishop: The Wrong Corner · White to move

FEN: 7k/5R2/6K1/8/8/8/8/6b1 w - - 0 1

The black king on h8 is trapped in the wrong corner — the same colour as the bishop on g1. White wins by forcing the bishop out of its safe squares behind the white king, then gaining the decisive tempo needed for mate.

Winning move

1.Rf1!

Mistake: 1.Rb7??

1.Rb7?? Bf8

After 1...Bf8 the king can no longer be forced back into the wrong corner. The drawing resource is gone for White.

Main line

1...Bh2 2.Rh1 Bg3 3.Rg1 Bh2 4.Rg2

The rook gradually removes the bishop's safe squares.

Continuation

4...Bd6 5.Rd2 Be7 6.Rc2

Warning

6.Rd7? Bf8 draws

Finish

6...Bf8 7.Rc8 #

Variation

1...Bd4 2.Rd1 Bb6 3.Rb1 Bc7 4.Rc1

Black can no longer prevent the mating threat. The rook has restricted the bishop to the fatal diagonal.

Key Idea

The rook wins by progressively restricting the bishop until it is forced onto vulnerable squares.

Ending 7

Rook vs Bishop: The Right Corner

The opposite corner completely changes the evaluation. The bishop has enough room to interpose, making the ending a theoretical draw.

Lesson

The Right Corner

The bishop can interpose on g8, giving Black drawing chances.

Position 1.18 · Rook vs Bishop: The Right Corner · White to move

FEN: 7k/R7/7K/8/8/1b6/8/8 w - - 0 1

The black king on h8 is in the right corner — the opposite colour to the bishop on b3. The bishop can interpose on g8 along the long diagonal, giving Black drawing chances.

Main line

1.Ra8+ Bg8

White must eventually release the pressure to avoid stalemate.

Continuation

2.Ra7 Bb3 3.Kg6 Bc4 4.Rh7+ Kg8 5.Rc7 Bd3+ 6.Kh6 Be4 =

Variation

2.Kg5 Kg7 3.Ra7+ Kh8 4.Kg6 Bc4 5.Ra3 Bd5 6.Rh3+ Kg8 7.Re3 Kh8 =

Another variation

3.Rh7+ Kg8 4.Rb7 Bd5 =

The bishop always has enough squares available along the long diagonal.

Warning

3...Be6??

fails because:

4.Rh7+ Kg8 5.Re7 wins

Key Idea

The correct corner gives the bishop enough defensive squares to hold the draw.

Ending 8

Rook vs Knight

Although Rook vs Knight is generally drawn, the knight must remain close to its king. If separated, the knight may be lost.

Lesson

Knight and King Together

Being driven to the edge is usually not fatal. The real danger comes when the knight becomes separated from its king.

Position 1.19 · Rook vs Knight: king and knight together · White to move

FEN: 8/8/8/8/8/3k4/r7/3NK3 w - - 0 1

The knight on e1 and the white king on f1 are together on the edge. The black rook on a2 and king on d3 press for separation. The main danger is the knight being driven away from its king.

Main line

1.Nf2+ Ke3 2.Nd1+ Kf3 3.Nc3 Rc2 4.Nd1

The knight returns to the king. This is the only temporary separation before king and knight reunite.

Variation

3.Kf1 Re2 4.Nc3

Again the knight rejoins the king. The defence holds as long as the two pieces stay within reach of each other.

Warning

4.Nd5??

The knight is separated from the king and becomes the target. The rook can pick it off.

Practical Rule

Against a rook, the knight should stay close to its king. A separated knight can become the target.

Chapter Checklist

  • Against a rook pawn, occupying the bishop-file squares beside the promotion corner also draws.
  • The wrong corner is losing in Rook vs Bishop.
  • The right corner usually draws because the bishop can interpose.
  • The rook wins by forcing the bishop off its safe squares.
  • In Rook vs Knight, keeping the king and knight together is the fundamental defensive principle.
  • A separated knight becomes the target for the rook.
