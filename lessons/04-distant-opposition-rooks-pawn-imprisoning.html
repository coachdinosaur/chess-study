Rook's Pawn, Reserve Moves & Imprisoning

Distant opposition as a defensive tool, reserve pawn moves as a tempo-losing weapon, the special drawing rules of the rook's pawn, and how the defending king can imprison the stronger king near the edge.

  • Use distant opposition to prevent the attacking king from reaching key squares.
  • Understand reserve pawn moves as a way to lose a tempo and force the defender to move first.
  • Recognise that the rook's pawn is governed by unique stalemate rules.
  • Know that a defending king in front of a rook's pawn almost always draws.
  • Appreciate that additional rook's pawns do not overcome the drawing mechanism.
  • Spot when the defending king can imprison the stronger king near the edge.
Lesson

The Distant Opposition

For a 4th-rank pawn, the key squares are two ranks ahead: b3, c3, and d3. Sometimes opposition alone is not enough. The defender can use distant opposition to prevent the attacking king from reaching the key squares.

Position 1.12 · The distant opposition · White to move

FEN: 8/8/8/1kp5/8/8/8/K7 w - - 0 1 · Key squares: b3, c3, d3

When both kings stand on the same file separated by an odd number of squares, they have distant opposition. Although Black can advance one rank, he cannot make further progress because White will immediately take the direct opposition.

Distant opposition, like direct opposition, can be lost when important squares are unavailable.

Winning move

1.Kb1!

Main line

1...Kb4 2.Kb2! c4 3.Kc2 c3 4.Kc1 Kb3 5.Kb1 c2+ 6.Kc1 Kc3 =

Variation

1...Kc4 2.Kc2

Key Idea

Distant opposition allows the defender to stop the attacking king from reaching the key squares even before the kings come into direct contact.

Lesson

Using Reserve Moves

The white king already occupies the key squares, but he still needs to outflank the opposing king. This becomes possible because White has a reserve move with the pawn.

Position 1.13 · Using reserve moves · White to move

FEN: 8/6k1/8/6K1/8/6P1/8/8 w - - 0 1 · Key squares: f5, g5, h5

By advancing the pawn, White gives the move to Black while preserving the winning king position.

Winning move

1.g4!

Main line

1...Kh7 2.Kf6 Kh8 3.Kg6 Kg8 4.g5! Kh7 5.Kf7 Kh6 6.g6+ Kh7 7.g7 1-0

Side Notes

  • If 1...Kf7 then 2.Kh6.
  • If 2...Kh6 then 3.g5+ Kh7 4.Kf7.
  • If 2...Kg8 then 3.Kg6 Kf8 4.Kh7.

Key Idea

Reserve pawn moves are valuable because they lose a tempo, forcing the defender to move first while preserving the attacking king's ideal position.

Ending 4

The Rook's Pawn

Everything learned so far applies to every pawn except the rook's pawn. Because of stalemate possibilities, rook's pawns require special treatment.

Special Nature

The rook's pawn is the most exceptional of all pawns. Its proximity to the edge of the board creates stalemate resources that make even winning-looking positions drawn. The general key-square and opposition rules do not apply in the same way.

Lesson

Defending King in Front of the Pawn

If the defending king stands directly in the pawn's path, the game is drawn regardless of whose move it is.

Position 1.14 · The rook's pawn: defending king in front of the pawn · White to move

FEN: 8/8/8/8/8/6kp/8/6K1 w - - 0 1

The black king on g3 stands directly in the path of the h-pawn. No matter what White tries, the pawn cannot promote.

Main line

1.Kh1 h2 stalemate

Key Idea

With a rook's pawn, if the defending king occupies the promotion path, promotion is impossible.

Lesson

Quantity Is Not Quality

This imaginary position illustrates how powerful the drawing mechanism is. Even six connected rook's pawns cannot overcome the defending king when it occupies the critical square.

Position 1.15 · Quantity is not quality · White to move

FEN: 8/7p/7p/7p/7p/6kp/7p/6K1 w - - 0 1

The black king on g3 blocks the path of all the h-pawns. White has six rook's pawns, yet none can promote.

Demonstration

1.Kh1 h2 stalemate

If White tries a different pawn:

1.h6 h5 stalemate

Or:

1.h4 h3+ 2.Kh1 h2 stalemate

The stronger side must eventually give up every pawn or remain unable to advance because every attempt ends in stalemate.

Key Idea

More pawns do not help if the fundamental rook-pawn drawing mechanism cannot be overcome.

Ending 5

Imprisoning the Stronger Side's King

Standing in the pawn's path is not the defender's only drawing method. Near the edge of the board, the defending king can imprison the stronger king and prevent progress.

Lesson

Imprisoning the Stronger Side's King

Black threatens ...Kb2, which would secure the pawn's promotion. White must react immediately.

Position 1.16 · Imprisoning the stronger side's king · White to move

FEN: 8/8/8/8/p7/1k6/3K4/8 w - - 0 1 · Key squares: c1, c2

The black king on b3 threatens ...Kb2, which would secure the pawn's promotion. White must react immediately.

Drawing move

1.Kc1!

White prevents ...Kb2 while threatening Kb1. The white king heads for the key squares c1 and c2 to block the black king's path.

Continuation

1...Ka2

This is a transition to the next section. The detailed continuation of the imprisonment theme follows in the next lesson.

Key Idea

Near the edge of the board, the defending king can imprison the stronger king and prevent progress, even when the pawn is far advanced.
