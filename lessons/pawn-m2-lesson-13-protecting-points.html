Protecting Points

Learn three important defensive ideas in chess: escaping a threat, protecting or supporting a piece, and blocking an attack.

1

Lesson Goal

In chess, you must notice when your pieces are under attack. If a piece is threatened, you can often save points by using a defensive move.

This lesson teaches three defensive ideas:

EscapeMove the threatened piece away.
Protection or supportDefend the threatened piece with another piece.
Block, shield, or coverPlace a piece in the line of attack.
Saving points is not only about capturing. It is also about keeping your own pieces safe.

Escape the Threat

Black has just moved 1...Qf6 and threatens the White rook.

Now White can use a defensive move called escape.

White moves:

2.Rb1

The rook escapes the Black queen's line of attack by moving away from danger.

Escape
Escape means moving your attacked piece to a safe square.
3

Activity: Escape Threat

Black threatens the White knight.

Question: Where should the knight move?

Show answer

The knight on f3 is attacked by the queen on f6. A knight on f3 can move to g1, h2, h4, g5, e5, d4, d2, or e1. Any safe square that is not attacked by the queen or the black king is a good escape. For example, Ne5 or Nd4 may keep the knight active while avoiding the queen.

4

Protection or Support

Sometimes, escape is impossible. But the threatened piece can still be saved if another piece protects it.

This defensive idea is called protection. It is also called support.

White moves:

2.Be3

Now the White knight is protected.

Protection
A protected piece may still be attacked, but the opponent cannot win it for free.
5

Activity: Protection

Black threatens the White bishop.

Question: What move protects the White bishop?

Show answer

The bishop on e4 is attacked by the queen on f6. White already has a knight on f3 that can support the bishop. The knight on f3 already protects e4, so the bishop is already defended. However, if the knight did not already guard e4, White could move a piece to protect it. A good defensive move adds support so the bishop is no longer hanging.

6

Block / Shield / Cover

Sometimes, you can stop a threat by putting a piece between the attacker and the target.

This is called a block. It may also be called a shield or cover.

Blocking can be used to:

  • protect a valuable piece,
  • stop a check,
  • stop a mate threat,
  • or stop an attack along a line.

In the example, White is threatened by the Black bishop.

White can block the attack by moving:

2.c3

A block only works if it really stops the line of attack. Always check what the opponent can capture next.

Question: Is this a good move?

Show answer

Not every blocking move is good. A move can block one threat but create a bigger problem. After choosing a block, always check whether the blocking piece can be captured or whether another important piece is still in danger.

✓

Review Questions

Use these questions to check your understanding.

What does escape mean?

Escape means moving a threatened piece away from danger to a safe square.

What does protection or support mean?

Protection or support means using another piece to defend the threatened piece.

What does block mean?

Block means placing a piece between the attacking piece and the target to stop the attack.

Why do these defensive moves help you save points?

They help you avoid losing pieces for free. Saving a rook, bishop, knight, queen, or pawn keeps your material safe.

Can a blocking move be bad?

Yes. A blocking move can be bad if the blocking piece can be captured easily or if the move creates a bigger threat.
