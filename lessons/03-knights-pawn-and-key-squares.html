Key Squares

Knight’s pawn stalemate resources, diagonal opposition, and the essential concept of key squares — the rules that tell us at a glance whether the pawn can promote.

  • Recognise knight’s pawn stalemate resources caused by the nearby edge.
  • Understand diagonal opposition in exceptional edge-board cases.
  • Know the key squares for a 5th-rank pawn and for a pawn not yet on the 5th.
  • Apply the rule: if the strong king occupies a key square, the pawn promotes.
  • Avoid pushing the pawn before the king has prepared the way.

Knight’s Pawn

We continue from the knight’s pawn preview of the previous chapter. White cannot take the opposition, but the nearby edge creates a special saving resource.

Position 1.8 · Knight’s pawn stalemate resource · White to move

FEN: 8/8/8/8/8/5kp1/7K/8 w - - 0 1

Let us have a look at the position. White cannot take the opposition. If the king moves in front of the pawn, he will have to retreat from his blocking position on the next move.

This position would be lost if Black had a central pawn, but with a knight’s pawn White can be saved thanks to a special detail.

The right move

1.Kh1!

This relative position of the kings is called diagonal opposition. Usually, diagonal opposition does not help much, but here it works because of the nearby edge.

Continuation

1...Kf2

This move would secure promotion with any other pawn, but now the white king is stalemated and the position is drawn.

Variation

1...Kg4 2.Kg2!=

Key Idea

With a knight’s pawn, the nearby edge can create stalemate resources that do not exist with central pawns.

Ending 3

Key Squares

When the pawn has not reached the 6th rank yet, the analysis grows in complexity, but there are very clear rules. If we know them, we can play the ending accurately and quickly know whether the pawn promotes or not. The essential concept here is that of key squares.

Key Principle

If the strong king occupies one of the key squares, the pawn promotes.

Lesson 1

Key Squares for a 5th-Rank Pawn

When the pawn is on the 5th rank, its key squares are the three squares directly in front of it. Since the white king has reached one of them, White wins.

Position 1.9 · Key squares for a 5th-rank pawn · White to move

FEN: 5k2/8/4K3/5P2/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1 · Key squares: e6, f6, g6

The marked squares e6, f6, and g6 are the key squares for the pawn on f5. The white king already occupies one of them, so White wins.

Winning move

1.Kf6!

Not 1.f6??

1.f6?? Ke8!

Black would take the opposition and draw. There is no need to hurry to push the pawn. The white king must pave the way first.

Continuation

1...Ke8

The black king has retreated from the pawn’s path. Now White secures promotion.

Main line

2.Kg7! Ke7 3.f6+ Ke6 4.f7 1-0

Now the pawn’s path is clear.

Extra Explanation

If, in the starting position, the white king had been on g6, 1.Kf6 would have led to the same winning position.

If the white king had been on f6, White wins with:

1.Ke6 Ke8 2.f6

White takes the opposition in the key situation: when the pawn reaches the 6th rank.

Were it Black’s turn in any of the three key-square examples, White would still win by one of two methods:

  1. supporting the pawn from the 7th rank, or
  2. taking the opposition.

Key Idea

White wins if his king reaches any key square.

Knight’s Pawn and Key Squares

Once again, the knight’s pawn poses extra difficulties, but the key-square rule still applies. We just need to be slightly more careful.

Position 1.10 · Knight’s pawn · White to move

FEN: 7k/5K2/8/6P1/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1

The king must go to the other side to avoid annoying stalemate tricks.

Winning move

1.Kg6!

The king must go to the other side to avoid annoying stalemate tricks.

Mistake

1.g6? stalemate

Note: 1.Kf6 does not throw away the win, but after accurate defence White may need to return to the starting position.

Main line

1...Kg8 2.Kh6 Kh8 3.g6

Now the king is on the correct side of the pawn, so promotion runs smoothly.

Continuation

3...Kg8 4.g7 Kf7 5.Kh7 1-0

Key Idea

For a knight’s pawn, the winning king often needs to stand on the correct side of the pawn to avoid stalemate tricks.

Lesson 3

Key Squares Before the Pawn Reaches the 5th Rank

If the pawn has not reached the 5th rank, its key squares are two ranks ahead. For example, for a pawn on the 4th rank, the key squares are on the 6th rank. The rule still applies: if the white king occupies one of those key squares, the pawn promotes.

Position 1.11 · Key squares when the pawn has not reached the 5th rank · White to move

FEN: 4k3/8/8/8/3PK3/8/8/8 w - - 0 1 · Key squares: c6, d6, e6

The marked squares c6, d6, and e6 are the key squares for the pawn on d4. In the diagram position, the white king can reach one of the key squares, but sometimes opposition makes the fight for those squares harder.

Winning move

1.Kd5!

White heads for the key square c6.

Mistake

1.Ke5?? Ke7!

After this, opposition prevents the white king from reaching the key squares.

Main line

1.Kd5! Ke7 2.Kc6

Once White has occupied one of the key squares, the pawn promotes.

Continuation

2...Kd8 3.Kd6 Kc8 4.Ke7 Kc7 5.d5 Kc8 6.d6 Kb7 7.d7 1-0

Key Idea

Before the pawn reaches the 5th rank, the strong king must aim for the key squares two ranks ahead of the pawn.
