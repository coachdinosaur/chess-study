Mate with the Double Rook

The double-rook mate, also called the rook roller, is one of the simplest checkmates to learn.

Starting Position (Rank method)

The plan is to push the Black king one row at a time until he is checkmated on the last rank. The two rooks take turns giving check.

White to move. White: King e1, Rooks a4 and b1. Black: King g5.

1. Rb5! 1... Kf6 2. Ra6+ Ke7 3. Rb7+ Kd8 4. Ra8#

2

Rook Roller Along Files

The rook takes charge of the e-file so the other rook can start the checks.

White to move. White: King a1, Rooks d1 and d2. Black: King f5.

Move: 1. Re2

3

Black Interferes

The Black king tries to interfere by stopping 3. Rg2+. White has two good solutions.

1... Kf4 2. Rf1+ Kg3

After 2...Kg3: White: King a1, Rooks e2 and f1. Black: King g3. White to move.

4

Option 1

White swings the rook away and then climbs back.

3. Re8! Kg2 4. Rf7

After 4... Kg3 White continues: 5. Rg8+ Kh4 6. Rh7#

5

Option 2

White doubles the rooks on the f-file and then rolls up.

3. Ref2! Kg4 4. Rg1+ Kh3

Finish: 5. Rfg2! Kh4 6. Rh1#

6

Activity Position

Solve the position with your coach. Mate Black using the rook roller.

White: King d2, Rooks c1 and f4. Black: King d5. White to move.

✓

Review Questions

Use these questions to check understanding.

What is the rook roller?

A double-rook checkmate method where the rooks take turns cutting off the king.

Why should the rooks avoid blocking each other?

If they stand on the wrong squares, one rook can stop the other rook from giving check.
