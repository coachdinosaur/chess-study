1

Activity Goal

This activity checks whether students can tell the difference between stalemate and non-stalemate positions.

Lesson focus
A stalemate happens only when the side to move is not in check and has no legal move.
2

How to Check for Stalemate

Follow these steps before looking at the answer key.

1
Check the king first.
If the king is in check, it is not stalemate.
2
Look for legal moves.
If the player has any legal move, it is not stalemate.
3
Use the stalemate rule.
Stalemate means the king is not in check, but the player has no legal move.
3

Answer Key

Use this section after trying the activity first.

A position is stalemate only when White is not in check and White has no legal move.
Position 1: YES

This is stalemate. The white king is not in check on h1, but it cannot move to g1, g2, or h2.

  • White is not in check.
  • White has no legal king move.
  • White has no other piece or pawn to move.
Position 2: YES

This is stalemate. The black pawn on f2 attacks g1, and the black king controls g2 and h2. The white king is not in check on h1.

  • White is not in check.
  • The pawn attacks g1.
  • The black king controls g2 and h2.
Position 3: YES

This is stalemate. The white bishop on g1 blocks the rook, so the white king is not in check. But the bishop cannot move because moving it would expose the king to the rook.

  • White is not in check because the bishop blocks the rook.
  • The king cannot move safely.
  • The bishop is pinned and has no legal move.
Position 4: NO

This is not stalemate. The white king cannot move, but White still has a legal pawn move: a3 or a4.

  • White is not in check.
  • The king has no safe square.
  • But White can move the pawn to a3 or a4.
5

Remember

Use the stalemate test in the correct order.

Check firstA checked king means it is not stalemate.
Legal moves matterAny legal move means it is not stalemate.
No legal moveIf the king is safe but cannot move and no other move exists, it is stalemate.
Stalemate is a drawNo player wins a stalemated game.
✓

Review Questions

Check the main ideas from this activity.

What two things must be true for stalemate?

The side to move must not be in check, and the side to move must have no legal move.

If the king is in check, can it be stalemate?

No. If the king is in check, the position is not stalemate.

Why is Position 4 not stalemate?

White still has a legal pawn move: a3 or a4.
