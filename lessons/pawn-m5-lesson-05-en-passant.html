What is En Passant?

En passant is a special pawn capture in chess.

The term en passant comes from French and means “in passing.” When an enemy pawn moves two squares forward from its starting square and lands next to your pawn, your pawn can capture it as if it had only moved one square forward. Your pawn moves diagonally to the square the enemy pawn passed through, and the enemy pawn is removed from the board.

Key idea
En passant captures the enemy pawn “in passing” — as it moves past your pawn.
2

The Three Conditions

All three conditions must be met for an en passant capture to be legal.

  1. The enemy pawn must have just moved two squares forward from its starting square. If it moved only one square, en passant is not possible.
  2. Your pawn must be on the fifth rank (for White) or the fourth rank (for Black). Your pawn must be on the adjacent file, next to the square the enemy pawn landed on.
  3. You must capture immediately on the very next move. If you make any other move first, the right to capture en passant is lost forever.
Condition 3 is the most commonly forgotten rule: the en passant capture is only available on the immediate next turn!

Example Diagram

4

Notation

En passant is written in standard algebraic notation.

The en passant capture is notated just like a normal pawn capture, followed by the optional annotation e.p. to indicate it was an en passant capture. The destination square is the square the capturing pawn moves to (the square the enemy pawn passed through), not the square the enemy pawn occupied.

exd6 e.p.

In this example, e is the file of the capturing pawn, x means captures, d6 is the destination square, and e.p. indicates the capture was en passant. Some players omit the e.p. and simply write exd6, but adding e.p. helps avoid confusion.

Remember
The capturing pawn does not move to the square where the enemy pawn sits. It moves to the square the enemy pawn passed through.
✓

Review Questions

Use these questions to check understanding.

What does “en passant” mean in French?

“In passing.”

How many squares must the enemy pawn move for en passant to be possible?

Two squares forward from its starting square.

On which move must you capture en passant?

Immediately on the very next move. If you make any other move first, the right is lost.

What square does the capturing pawn move to in en passant?

The square the enemy pawn passed through (not the square where the enemy pawn landed).
