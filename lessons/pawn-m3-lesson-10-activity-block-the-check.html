Activity: Block the Check

Question: What is White’s move to block the check? Look at each position and find the move that blocks the checking line.

Find the checking lineThe black bishop checks along the d5–e4–f3–g2–h1 diagonal.
Find a blocking squareThe useful blocking squares are e4, f3, and g2.
Move the blockerPlace a white piece or pawn between the bishop and the king.
Escape the checkAfter the block, the bishop no longer attacks the king.

Activity Goal

This activity checks whether students can escape check by blocking the checking line.

Lesson focus
The white king is in check from the black bishop on d5. White must place a piece or pawn on the diagonal between the bishop and the king.
2

How to Do the Activity

Follow these steps before looking at the answer key.

1
Find the checking line.
The black bishop on d5 checks the white king on h1 along the diagonal d5–e4–f3–g2–h1.
2
Find a blocking square.
A white piece can block the check by moving to e4, f3, or g2.
3
Move the blocker.
The answer move should place a white piece or pawn between the bishop and the king.

Answer Key

Use this section after trying the activity first.

Use this section after trying the activity first.

Accuracy note: some exact positions allow more than one legal blocking move. The answer key gives the clearest intended blocking move and notes alternatives where needed.
Position 1: Nf3

The white knight moves from h4 to f3. The knight blocks the bishop’s diagonal line to the king.

Ng2 also blocks the line in this exact position.
Position 2: Bg2

The white bishop moves from h3 to g2. The bishop blocks the checking line between d5 and h1.

If the two-square pawn move is allowed in your lesson sequence, e4 also blocks the line.
Position 3: Nf3

The white knight moves from h2 to f3. The knight blocks the bishop’s diagonal line to the king.

If the two-square pawn move is allowed in your lesson sequence, e4 also blocks the line.
Position 4: f3

The white pawn moves from f2 to f3. The pawn blocks the bishop’s diagonal line to the king.

e4 is also a legal block if White may move the e-pawn two squares.
5

Remember

Blocking is one of the three basic ways to escape check.

Block the lineMove a white piece or pawn between the checking bishop and the king.
Use a legal squareThe blocker must land on the checking line, such as e4, f3, or g2.
The king becomes safeAfter the block, the checking piece no longer attacks the king.
Check firstWhen in check, your move must answer the check immediately.
✓

Review Questions

Check the main ideas from this activity.

What does it mean to block a check?

It means placing a piece between the checking piece and the king so the king is no longer attacked.

Which black piece gives check in this activity?

The black bishop on d5 gives check along the diagonal to the white king on h1.

What are the blocking squares on the diagonal?

The blocking squares are e4, f3, and g2.
