Coach Dinosaur Chess Course

Pawn Level

Beginner chess curriculum. Work through each module to learn the game from the board up.

Module 1 Board, Notation, Setup, Basic Rules, and Piece Values Completed
  1. 1. Introducing the Game of Chess

    What chess is, where it came from, and why it is learned around the world.
  2. 2. The Chessboard

    64 squares, alternating colors, and the rule: white square on the right.
  3. 3. Files, Ranks, and Diagonals

    Learn the three ways the board is divided and how squares are grouped.
  4. 4. Scorekeeping: Algebraic Notation

    Learn square names, move notation, captures, and examples like Kf2, Nd3, and Nxd3.
  5. 5. Algebraic Notation Examples

    See real example moves and the rule that pawns have no letter in notation.
  6. 6. Capturing a Piece

    How a capture works, how to read Nxd3, and why captures matter.
  7. 7. Chess Terms

    Kingside, queenside, whiteside, and blackside — the four board areas.
  8. 8. Setting Up the Chessboard

    Place kings, queens, rooks, knights, bishops, and pawns in the correct starting squares.
  9. 9. Basic Chess Rules

    Two players, taking turns, White moves first, and the standard starting position.
  10. 10. The Chessmen

    32 chessmen, 16 per side, and the difference between a piece and a pawn.
  11. 11. Piece Values

    The beginner point value of each piece and why the king has none.
Module 2 How the Pieces Move Ready
  1. 1. How to Move the King

    Understand why the King is the most important piece and how it moves one square in any direction.
  2. 2. How to Capture with the King

    Learn how the King captures one square away from any position on the board.
  3. 3. How to Move the Knight

    Learn how the knight moves in an L-shape and is the only piece that jumps over others.
  4. 4. How to Capture with the Knight

    Learn how the knight captures with an L-shaped move, written as Nxe4.
  5. 5. How to Move the Pawn

    Learn how pawns move one square forward, or two squares from their starting position.
  6. 6. Pawn Capture

    Learn how pawns capture one square diagonally forward, not straight ahead.
  7. 7. Pawn Promotion

    Learn how a pawn promotes to a queen, rook, bishop, or knight upon reaching the final rank.
  8. 8. Activity: Practice Moving the King, Knight, and Pawn

    Practice finding legal moves with the king, knight, and pawn.
  9. 9. How to Move the Rook

    Learn how the rook moves in straight lines along files and ranks.
  10. 10. How to Capture with the Rook

    Learn how the rook captures in a straight line, written as Rxe6.
  11. 11. How to Move the Bishop

    Learn how the bishop moves diagonally and always stays on the same color square.
  12. 12. How to Move the Queen

    Learn how the queen moves like a rook and bishop combined in any direction.
  13. 13. Activity: Practice Moving the Rook, Bishop, and Queen

    Practice finding legal moves and captures with the rook, bishop, and queen.
  14. 13b. Protecting Points

    Learn three defensive ideas: escape, protect, and block.
Module 3 Check, Checkmate, and Draws Completed
  1. 1. How to Win a Chess Game

    How a chess game is won, and why capturing is the path to victory.
  2. 2. Attack

    What an attack is, how Qe3 attacks the black queen on e8, and how attack differs from capture.
  3. 3. Check

    What check means, why the king is never captured, and how a bishop can check along a diagonal.
  4. 4. Illegal Move

    Illegal moves, the king rule, and why Ke3 is illegal when it moves into check.
  5. 5. Escaping a Check

    Three beginner ways to escape check: capture, block, or run away.
  6. 6. How Do You Win a Chess Game?

    Checkmate, how to win a game, and why the enemy king must have no escape.
  7. 7. Stalemate

    What stalemate means, how it differs from checkmate, and why stalemate is a draw.
  8. 8. Activity: Check the King

    Activity: find Black's checking move in each position.
  9. 9. Activity: Capture the Checker

    Activity: find the move that captures the checking bishop and escapes check.
  10. 10. Activity: Block the Check

    Activity: find the move that blocks the checking bishop line and escapes check.
  11. 11. Activity: Is This a Checkmate?

    Activity: decide whether each position is checkmate by checking for check and legal escapes.
  12. 12. Activity: Is This a Stalemate?

    Activity: decide whether each position is stalemate by checking for check and legal moves.
Module 4 Basic Checkmate & Stalemate Ready
  1. 1. Checkmate with a Rook

    Learn why the enemy king must be pushed to the edge and how the king and rook work together.
  2. 2. Rook Mating Pattern 1

    See a basic rook checkmate where the rook gives check and the king controls escape squares.
  3. 3. Rook Mating Pattern 2

    Learn how the corner helps trap the king in another basic rook mate.
  4. 4. Rook Mate Example 1

    Use the shrinking box method to push the Black king to the edge of the board.
  5. 5. Rook Mate Example 2

    A simpler example because the Black king is already on the edge of the board.
  6. 6. Checkmate with the Queen

    Use the queen and the shrinking box method, but be careful not to stalemate.
  7. 7. Mate with the Double Rook

    Learn the rook roller, one of the simplest checkmates, using two rooks.
  8. 8. Stalemate

    What stalemate means and why it is a draw, not a win.
  9. 9. Not a Stalemate

    A position is not stalemate if the player has at least one legal move.
  10. 10. Activity: Checkmate or Stalemate?

    Practice deciding whether each position is checkmate or stalemate.
