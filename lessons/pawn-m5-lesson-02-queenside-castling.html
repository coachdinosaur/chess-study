Queenside Castling

What Is Queenside Castling?

Queenside castling uses the rook on the a-file.

The White king moves from e1 to c1, and the a1 rook moves from a1 to d1. For Black, the king moves from e8 to c8, and the a8 rook moves to d8.

Notation
Queenside castling is written as O-O-O: three capital letter Os separated by dashes.
2

The Move

Once the squares between the king and rook are clear, the king moves two squares toward the rook.

The rook then crosses to d1 and finishes beside the king. The queen does not take part in the move.

In queenside castling, the White king moves from e1 to c1, and the rook moves from a1 to d1.
3

Kingside vs Queenside

The king always moves two squares. The rook travels a different distance depending on the side.

  • Kingside (O-O): king e1→g1; rook h1→f1.
  • Queenside (O-O-O): king e1→c1; rook a1→d1.
  • On both sides, the king moves exactly two squares. On the queenside, the rook moves three squares.
Choose the side where the king will be safer. Castling is a safety decision, not a move that must always be played.

Review Questions

Use these questions to check understanding.

Where does the White king move in queenside castling?

From e1 to c1.

Where does the a1 rook move?

From a1 to d1.

How many squares does the king move?

Exactly two squares, just as in kingside castling.

What is the notation for queenside castling?

O-O-O.
