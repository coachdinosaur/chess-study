Activity: Is This a Checkmate?

Question: White to move. Is this a checkmate? Answer each position with YES or NO.

Check the king firstIf the king is not in check, the position is not checkmate.
Try to captureIf White can legally capture the checking piece, answer NO.
Try to block or runIf White can block the check or move safely, answer NO.
Answer clearlyWrite YES for checkmate and NO if any escape exists.

Activity Goal

This activity checks whether students can decide if a checked king is truly checkmated.

Lesson focus
A checkmate happens only when the king is in check and has no legal way to escape.
2

How to Check for Checkmate

Follow these steps before looking at the answer key.

1
Is the king in check?
If the king is not in check, it is not checkmate.
2
Can White capture the checker?
If White can capture the checking piece legally, it is not checkmate.
3
Can White block or run away?
If White can block the check or move the king to a safe square, it is not checkmate.
3

Answer Key

Use this section after trying the activity first.

Checkmate means the king is in check and has no legal escape.
Position 1: YES

This is checkmate. The black rook checks the white king along the 1st rank, and the white king has no safe square.

  • White is in check.
  • White cannot capture the rook.
  • White cannot block the rook.
  • White cannot move the king to a safe square.
Position 2: YES

This is checkmate. The bishop on d5 checks the white king, while the second bishop and black king cover the escape squares.

  • White is in check from the bishop on d5.
  • The king cannot run to g1, g2, or h2.
  • White has no piece that can capture the bishop or block the check.
Position 3: NO

This is not checkmate. The black pawn gives check on h1, but the white king can escape to g1.

  • White is in check from the black pawn on g2.
  • The white king cannot capture the pawn because the black king protects g2.
  • But the white king can move to g1, so it is not checkmate.
Position 4: NO

This is not checkmate. The black queen gives check along the diagonal to h1, but the white king can escape to g1.

  • White is in check from the queen on d5.
  • The queen attacks h1 along d5–e4–f3–g2–h1.
  • But g1 is a legal escape square, so it is not checkmate.
5

Remember

Use the checkmate test in the correct order.

Check firstIf the king is not attacked, it is not checkmate.
Capture the checkerIf the checking piece can be captured legally, it is not checkmate.
Block or runIf White can block the line or move the king safely, it is not checkmate.
No escapeCheckmate means every legal escape has failed.
✓

Review Questions

Check the main ideas from this activity.

What two things must be true for checkmate?

The king must be in check, and the checked player must have no legal way to escape.

If the king can move to a safe square, is it checkmate?

No. A legal safe king move means the position is not checkmate.

Why are Positions 3 and 4 answered NO?

In both positions, White is in check, but the white king can escape to g1.
