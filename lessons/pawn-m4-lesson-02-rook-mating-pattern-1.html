Rook Mating Pattern 1

This is a basic rook checkmate pattern. The rook gives check, while the king controls the escape squares.

1

The Position

White has a rook on a8 and a king on d6. Black has a king on d8.

Black is on the edge of the board.

Checkmate position

Black king on d8 is trapped. White king on d6 controls escape squares.

2

What the Rook Does

The White rook attacks along the 8th rank.

Because the Black king is on d8, the rook gives check from a8.

3

What the King Does

The White king controls c7, d7, and e7.

These are the escape squares near the Black king.

The red X marks show squares the Black king cannot move to.

4

Why It Is Checkmate

The Black king is in check from the rook.

  • The Black king cannot move to any safe square.
  • The Black king cannot capture the rook.
  • The Black king cannot block the check.
Therefore, this is checkmate.

Review Questions

Use these questions to check understanding.

Which piece gives check?

The White rook.

Which piece controls the red X squares?

The White king.

Why can the Black king not escape?

The rook gives check and the White king controls the escape squares.
