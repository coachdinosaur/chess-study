Knight and Bishop vs a Lone King

Coordinate the king, bishop, and knight; force the enemy king away from the wrong corner; follow the W-shaped knight route; and finish in the bishop-colored corner.

Choose the CornerMate only in a corner controlled by the bishop. Here, the dark-squared targets are a1 and h8.
Bring the KingThe attacking king protects the knight and removes nearby escape squares.
Reach the EdgeUse the king and bishop to reduce the lone king’s territory before starting the W route.

Wait and LimitThe bishop often makes quiet waiting or limiting moves so the knight can continue safely.
Part 1

Target Squares and the Starting Position

The guide is designed to be easy to follow rather than mathematically fastest. First identify the correct mating corners, then coordinate the pieces to force the lone king toward one of them.

Target cornersa1 and h8. Both are dark squares controlled by White’s bishop.
Wrong cornersa8 and h1. The bishop cannot control these corners, so the standard mate cannot be completed there.

Knight-and-Bishop Mate Checklist

Use this sequence when practicing the ending:

  • Color: Which two corners match the bishop’s square color?
  • Approach: Is the attacking king close enough to protect the knight?
  • Edge: Can the king and bishop force the defender to the edge?
  • Wrong corner: Which knight square removes the wrong corner?
  • W route: What is the knight’s next protected zigzag square?
  • Wait: Does the bishop need a waiting move before the knight can continue?
  • Limit: Which bishop move removes another escape square?
  • Finish: Are the king, bishop, and knight covering every flight square?
Review

Check Your Understanding

1. Which are the correct mating corners in this lesson?

a1 and h8, because the bishop moves on dark squares.

2. Why is a8 the wrong corner?

The bishop cannot control a8, so the standard bishop-and-knight mating construction cannot be completed there.

3. What is the purpose of 11.Nc7?

The knight controls a8 and prevents the Black king from returning to the wrong corner.

4. Why are bishop waiting moves necessary?

They preserve the restriction and pass the move to Black when the knight cannot continue safely or immediately.

5. What is the final mating move?

30.Bb2#.
