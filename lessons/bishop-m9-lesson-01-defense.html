Part 1

What Is Defense in Chess?

Defense includes preventing checkmate, protecting threatened material, avoiding tactics, removing weaknesses, preserving strengths, simplifying, and counterattacking. Its purpose is to equalize or turn the attack against the opponent.

Defend the position, not only one object

A move that saves one piece but ruins king safety or pawn structure may merely exchange one problem for a larger one.

Part 2

Fundamental Defensive Moves

Blocking

Place a piece between the attacker and its target.

Escaping or retreating

Move the threatened piece or king to a safer square.

Supporting

Add another defender to a piece, pawn, or critical square.

Active defense

Attack the opponent’s queen, center, king, or loose material while meeting the threat.

Part 3

Defending Against a Pawn Storm

Position 1 · Black to move · PDF pages 5–6

White’s last move was g4, preparing g5 and possibly f4 or h4.

The pawn storm aims to drive away the knight on f6 and open kingside files. Black should compare several defensive methods instead of choosing the most passive-looking move by reflex.

Attack the base or the center

Flank attacks often become weaker when the defender strikes the center before the attacking pieces are ready.

Central counterattack
1...d5

or 1...e5, challenging White’s center.

Develop and challenge
1...Nc6

Develop the queenside knight and increase pressure on White’s centralized pieces.

Stop the advance
1...h6 2.g5 hxg5 3.Bxg5

White gains open lines, so the resulting structure must be calculated.

Allow and regroup
1...Nc6 2.g5 Nd7

The f6-knight may later reach c5 or e5.

Queenside counterplay
1...a6

followed by ...b5.

Part 4

Defense Against a Dangerous Attack

Position 2 · Black to move · PDF pages 7–8

White threatens Qxg7 and then the rook on h8.

Black should prefer a defense that solves the immediate problem while gaining time or creating counterplay.

Two-purpose defense

A move that attacks the queen or center while defending is often stronger than a purely passive move.

Move the pawn with tempo
1...g6

The queen is attacked, but f6 and h6 become weaker.

King protection
1...Kf8

The king protects g7 but forfeits castling.

Defense with tempo
1...f5

Black attacks the queen while interrupting White’s plan.

Counterthreat
1...Ne7 2.Qxg7 Rg8 3.Qxh7 cxd4

Black opens the c-file and creates ideas such as ...Qxe5+ or ...Qxc3+.

Central counterplay
1...f6

The move challenges e5, though it does not directly defend g7.

Part 5

Combine Defense with Other Skills

Good defense combines tactical awareness with king safety, material, pawn structure, development, weak-square evaluation, exchanges, and calculation. Understanding attacking principles also helps the defender recognize premature attacks and the moments when counterplay is possible.

Study strong defenders

The source recommends analyzing games by champions known for resilient defense and counterplay, including Steinitz, Lasker, Capablanca, Botvinnik, Tal, Fischer, Karpov, and Kasparov.

Defense Exercises

Position 3 · White to move · PDF page 10

Black threatens ...Ng4, ...Qh2+, and ...Qxf2#.

White must examine all of Black’s checks, not merely stop the advertised knight move. Candidate defensive ideas include f3, Be1, or active forcing counterplay.

Show calculation checklist

  • Identify the queen, knight, bishop, and rook participating in the attack.
  • Reject moves that stop ...Ng4 but allow another immediate mating line.
  • Compare direct protection with forcing counterplay.

Defensive Checklist

Use this sequence when facing an attack:

  • Threat: What exactly is threatened, including checks and mating ideas?
  • Protection: Can the target be supported, moved, or blocked?
  • Exchange: Can an important attacker be removed?
  • Counterplay: Can you attack the queen, center, king, or loose material with tempo?
  • Structure: Which defensive move creates the fewest new weaknesses?
  • Calculation: Does the defense survive the opponent’s strongest continuation?
Review

Check Your Understanding

1. What is the purpose of defense?

To prevent immediate damage, equalize the position, or turn the opponent’s attack into counterplay.

2. What are the main defensive methods?

Protection, exchange, counterattack, maneuver, blocking, retreating, and supporting.

3. Why is central counterplay useful against a pawn storm?

A flank attack may lose force if the center opens before the attacker is ready.

4. What is a defense with tempo?

A move that meets the threat while attacking an enemy piece or creating another urgent problem.

5. Why calculate every legal response to check?

Some legal moves still allow immediate mate or decisive material loss.
