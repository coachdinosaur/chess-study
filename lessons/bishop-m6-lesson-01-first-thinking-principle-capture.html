Part 1

Two Meanings of Capture

Coach Dinosaur uses the word capture both for an ordinary move and for the first thinking principle. The capitalization may be subtle. The material consequences are rather less subtle.

Ordinary capture A move such as Nxd3, where the knight removes the enemy piece on d3 and occupies that square.
Capture thinking principle First ask how to avoid losing material and how to gain material in the current position.

The principle starts the analysis

It narrows the candidate moves, but it does not replace calculation. Every exchange still has to be followed to its final material and tactical result.

Part 2

Avoid Losing Material

Black must deal with the pressure on the knight on f5. Moving the knight, defending it, or exchanging pieces all deserve consideration.

Position 1 · Black to move · PDF page 4

The bishop on d3 attacks f5. The queen on c2 stands behind it, while the bishop on d7 protects the knight.

The bishop directly attacks the knight. The queen forms a battery behind the bishop and may recapture on f5 after the bishop exchanges. If the knight simply leaves, White may also gain the h7-pawn with Bxh7+.

Part 3

Compare the Defensive Candidates

A move that appears to solve the first problem may still lose material several moves later.

Candidate A: add protection with 1...Qf6

1...Qf6 2.Bxf5 Bxf5 3.Qb3 Qc6 4.Nd4 Qd7 5.Nxf5 Qxf5 6.Qxd5
Source corrections: the PDF says that 1...Qf6 supports a pawn, but it actually adds protection to the knight on f5. It also prints 6.Qxd7, which is illegal from b3. The coherent legal move is 6.Qxd5.

Black saves the knight initially but eventually loses the d5-pawn.

Candidate B: 1...g6

1...g6 2.Qd2 Qf6 3.Ne5 Qd6 4.Nxd7 Qxd7 5.g3

This line produces the better material outcome. The lesson is not that 1...g6 is visible by magic. It becomes the leading candidate after the inferior line is calculated and rejected.

Calculation rule

Do not stop when the attacked piece is saved. Continue through the exchanges until the material balance and resulting position can be evaluated.

Part 4

Calculate Before Taking the Queen

The Black queen on c2 looks available, but the move order determines whether White gains material or walks into a forcing discovered check.

Position 2 · White to move · PDF page 6

The pawn on d4 can move with check by opening the bishop’s diagonal from b6 to g1.

The tempting bishop capture allows Black to activate the d-pawn with tempo. White should exchange queens first and only then recapture with the bishop.

Move-order warning

Two captures that seem equivalent may produce completely different tactical positions.

The losing choice:

1.Bxc2? d3+ 2.Qf2 Bxf2+ 3.Rxf2 Rxc2 4.Rd1

The better choice:

1.Qxc2 Rxc2 2.Bxc2 d3+ 3.Kf1 dxc2 4.Ke2

Part 5

Eliminate Choices and Evaluate the Result

Thinking principles reduce the number of moves that deserve full analysis. They do not excuse the player from calculating three, five, or sometimes more moves.

Position 3 · After 4.Ke2 · Black to move · PDF page 7

White has chosen the better exchange sequence. The c2-pawn is advanced, but it is not an immediate promotion threat.

White has gained a small material advantage and has enough activity to contain the passed pawn. The tactical resource ...d3+ was unavoidable, but the correct move order limited its effect.

Final-position test

Evaluate the material, passed pawns, king safety, and activity only after the forcing sequence is complete.

What the first principle requires

  • Count attackers and defenders accurately.
  • Know approximate material values.
  • Recognize tactical ideas such as discovered checks.
  • Visualize exchanges several moves ahead.
  • Reject losing candidates and compare the survivors.

Capture Principle Checklist

Use this material scan before committing to an important move:

  • Attacks: Which pieces are attacked, loose, or overloaded?
  • Defenses: Can the attacked piece move, gain protection, or exchange favorably?
  • Captures: What captures and recaptures are available to both sides?
  • Tactics: Does the line contain a check, discovered attack, pin, fork, or passed-pawn idea?
  • Material: What is the balance after the forcing line ends?
  • Elimination: Which candidate moves can be rejected immediately?
Review

Check Your Understanding

1. What are the two meanings of capture in this module?

An ordinary capture removes an enemy piece. The Capture thinking principle begins by checking how to avoid losing material and how to gain material.

2. Why is the queen on c2 relevant to the knight on f5?

The queen stands behind the bishop on d3 and may recapture along the diagonal after the bishop exchanges on f5.

3. Why is the printed 6.Qxd7 illegal?

The White queen is on b3 and cannot capture d7. The coherent legal correction is 6.Qxd5.

4. Why is 1.Bxc2? inaccurate in the second example?

It permits 1...d3+, a discovered check that begins a forcing sequence and leaves White with the inferior material result.

5. Does the Capture principle replace calculation?

No. It identifies material problems and removes bad candidates, but the remaining lines must still be calculated and evaluated.
