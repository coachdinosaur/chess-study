Castling: Keeping Your King Safe

Castling is the only move in chess that moves two pieces at once: the king and one rook. It helps keep the king safe and brings the rook into play.

What Is Castling?

Castling is a special move involving the king and one rook. It is the only move where two pieces move during the same turn.

In castling, the king moves exactly two squares toward the rook. The rook then moves to the square immediately beside the king, on the other side.

Key fact
Castling counts as one move, not two. Move the king first when performing it on a physical board.
2

Kingside Castling

Kingside castling is also called short castling and is written as O-O.

The White king moves from e1 to g1, and the h1 rook moves from h1 to f1. For Black, the king moves from e8 to g8, and the h8 rook moves to f8.

3

Why Castle?

Castling has two big benefits: it moves the king toward safety and activates the rook.

After castling, the king is usually protected by a wall of pawns. The rook also moves toward the center, where it can join the game more quickly.

After kingside castling, White's king is on g1, usually protected by pawns on f2, g2, and h2. The rook on f1 is ready to help.
✓

Review Questions

Use these questions to check understanding.

How many pieces move during castling?

Two: the king and one rook.

In kingside castling, where does the White king move?

From e1 to g1.

Where does the h1 rook move?

From h1 to f1.

What is the notation for kingside castling?

O-O.
