Castling: Keeping Your King Safe

Castling is the only move in chess that moves two pieces at once: the king and the rook. It helps keep the king safe and brings the rook into play.

What Is Castling?

Castling is a special move involving the king and a rook. It is the only move where two pieces move at the same time.

In castling, the king moves two squares toward the rook, and then the rook jumps over the king to the square beside it.

Key fact
Castling is counted as one move, not two. The king always moves first when castling.
2

Kingside Castling

Kingside castling is also called "short castling" and is written as O-O.

The king moves from e1 to g1, and the h1 rook moves from h1 to f1. For Black, the king moves from e8 to g8, and the h8 rook moves to f8.

3

Why Castle?

Castling has two big benefits: it moves the king to a safer position and activates the rook.

After castling, the king is usually protected by a wall of pawns. The rook also moves toward the center where it becomes more useful.

After kingside castling, White's king is on g1, protected by pawns on f2, g2, and h2. The rook on f1 is ready to help attack.
✓

Review Questions

Use these questions to check understanding.

How many pieces move during castling?

Two: the king and one rook.

In kingside castling, where does the White king move to?

The king moves from e1 to g1.

What is the notation for kingside castling?

O-O.
