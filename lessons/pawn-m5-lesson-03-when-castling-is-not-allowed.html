When Castling Is Not Allowed

Castling is a special move, and five requirements must be met before you may use it.

King movedYou cannot castle if the king has already moved.
Rook movedYou cannot castle with a rook that has moved.
King in checkYou cannot castle out of check.
Through checkThe king cannot pass through a square under attack.
Contents
1. King or Rook Moved 2. King in Check 3. Through Check 4. Pieces in the Way Review Questions
1

King or Rook Has Moved

Once the king has moved, you lose the right to castle for the entire game.

Similarly, if a rook has moved, you cannot castle with that rook. The other rook may still be available if it has not moved.

If you move your king and then move it back, castling is still not allowed. The right is lost forever once the king moves.
2

King in Check

You cannot castle if your king is in check.

You must first escape the check by moving the king, blocking, or capturing. After the check is resolved, you might castle later if the king has not moved.

3

Through Check

The king cannot pass through a square that is attacked by an enemy piece.

For kingside castling, the f1 square (or f8 for Black) must be safe. For queenside castling, the d1 and c1 squares (or d8 and c8) must be safe.

Important
Only the king's path matters. The rook can pass through an attacked square. For example, in queenside castling, the rook passes over b1, which may be under attack — that is allowed.
4

Pieces in the Way

There must be no pieces between the king and the rook you want to castle with.

For kingside castling, the f1 and g1 squares (or f8 and g8) must be empty. For queenside castling, the b1, c1, and d1 squares (or b8, c8, d8) must be empty.

All five requirements must be met for castling to be legal: 1) The king has not moved. 2) The chosen rook has not moved. 3) The king is not in check. 4) The king does not cross or finish on an attacked square. 5) No pieces stand between the king and rook.
✓

Review Questions

Use these questions to check understanding.

If your king has moved once, can you castle later?

No. Once the king moves, castling is lost for the whole game.

Can you castle if your king is in check?

No. You must deal with the check first.

Does the rook have to avoid attacked squares during castling?

No. Only the king must avoid moving through check. The rook can pass through an attacked square.
