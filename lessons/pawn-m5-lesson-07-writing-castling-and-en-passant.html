Castling Notation

Castling has a special notation that looks different from normal moves.

In algebraic notation, castling is written with the uppercase letter O (not the number zero).

O-O Kingside castling
O-O-O Queenside castling

The letter O represents the visual shape of the move. Kingside castling uses two Os because the king moves two squares. Queenside castling uses three Os because the rook moves three squares.

Why Os?
The Os are used because they look like the circular pattern of the castling move on the board. Some old notation systems also wrote it as 0-0 and 0-0-0 using zeros.
2

En Passant Notation

En passant captures are written just like normal pawn captures, with e.p. added at the end.

For example, if a white pawn on e5 captures a black pawn that just moved from d7 to d5, the notation is exd6 e.p.

exd6 e.p.

e = the file of the capturing pawn · x = captures · d6 = the destination square · e.p. = en passant

The e.p. at the end tells the reader that this was an en passant capture, which is unusual because the capturing pawn does not end up on the square of the captured pawn.

Examples

Here are some example positions with their correct notation.

1
White castles kingside

The white king moves from e1 to g1. The rook moves from h1 to f1. Written as O-O.

2
Black castles queenside

The black king moves from e8 to c8. The rook moves from a8 to d8. Written as O-O-O.

3
White captures en passant

Black pawn moves d7-d5. White pawn on e5 captures it on d6. Written as exd6 e.p..

4
Black captures en passant

White pawn moves g2-g4. Black pawn on f4 captures it on g3. Written as fxg3 e.p..

Always use uppercase O, not the number zero, when writing castling.

Review Questions

Use these questions to check understanding.

How do you write kingside castling?

O-O (the letter O, not zero).

How do you write queenside castling?

O-O-O.

What does "e.p." mean after a move?

It means the capture was made en passant.

Is castling written with the letter O or the number 0?

The letter O.
