Pawn Level · Lesson 2

The Chessboard

64 squares, alternating colors, and the most important rule for setting up.

In this lesson you will learn:

  • How many squares are on the chessboard
  • How the squares alternate between light and dark
  • The rule for placing the board correctly
1

64 Squares

The chessboard is a square grid made up of 64 smaller squares — 8 rows of 8. Each square is the same size.

The squares alternate between two colors: light and dark. No two squares of the same color ever touch side by side.

An empty chessboard: 64 squares in alternating light and dark.

2

The Most Important Rule

When you place the chessboard in front of you, the bottom-right corner must always be a light square.

Notice the bottom-right corner is a light square (highlighted).

White on the Right!

Before beginning a game, always check: the bottom-right square should be light. This is the first thing to remember when setting up any chessboard.

3

Light and Dark Squares

The board is divided equally: 32 light squares and 32 dark squares. Together they form the checkered pattern you see on every chessboard.

The board has no special center square. Instead, the four squares in the very middle of the board form the center — two light and two dark.

Remember

  • The chessboard has 64 squares (8 × 8).
  • Squares alternate between light and dark.
  • There are 32 light squares and 32 dark squares.
  • The bottom-right corner must always be a light square — White on the right!
