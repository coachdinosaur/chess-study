How to Capture with the King

A capture means your chessman takes the place of an enemy chessman and removes it from the board. The King captures the same way it moves: one square at a time.

1

Lesson Goal

Learn how the King captures an enemy chessman.

Key idea
The King captures by moving one square onto an enemy piece or pawn.
2

How the King Captures

A capture means moving your piece to a square occupied by an enemy piece and removing that enemy piece from the chessboard.

Same as movingThe King captures the same way it moves.
One square onlyThe King can capture only one square away.
Any directionThe King may capture forward, backward, sideways, or diagonally if the move is legal.
3

King Capture Example

White King: e4. Black Pawn: e5. The White King can move from e4 to e5 and capture the Black Pawn.

Before the capture

The White King is on e4. The Black Pawn is one square away on e5.

After the capture

The King moves to e5. The Black Pawn is removed from the board.

Captured piece: Black pawn
4

Remember

Check these points before you capture with the King.

One squareThe King captures only one square away.
Same movementThe King captures the same way it moves.
Remove the pieceThe enemy piece is removed from the board.
Stay safeThe King must not move into danger.
✓

Review Questions

Use these questions to check understanding.

1. What is a capture?

A capture is when your chessman moves to an enemy chessman’s square and removes that enemy chessman from the board.

2. How far can the King capture?

The King can capture one square away.

3. In the example, where does the King move?

The King moves from e4 to e5.

4. What piece is captured?

The White King captures the Black Pawn.

5. Does the King capture differently from how it moves?

No. The King captures the same way it moves.
