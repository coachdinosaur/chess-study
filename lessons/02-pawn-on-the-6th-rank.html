King + Pawn vs King · Ending 2

The Pawn on the 6th Rank

Opposition is the decisive idea when a pawn nears promotion. This chapter explores when the pawn wins, when the defender holds, and how king manoeuvres around the 6th rank decide the result.

  • Know when a pawn on the 6th rank promotes and when it does not.
  • Understand opposition as the deciding factor in pawn-on-6th positions.
  • Recognise when to delay the pawn push and ask who will have the opposition.
  • Take the opposition with king manoeuvres to force promotion.
  • Spot when the defender loses the opposition because a key square is out of reach.
  • Be aware that edge pawns (knight’s, rook’s) carry stalemate risks.
Lesson 1

The Pawn Wins When the Path Is Clear

The first important position to fix in our minds occurs when the pawn has reached the 6th rank and is only two steps away from promotion. Despite the proximity of the 8th rank, we still need to examine the key details.

Position 1.4 · Pawn on the 6th rank · White to move

FEN: 3k4/8/5PK1/8/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1

In this position, the pawn can take its last two steps almost automatically because the white king controls the key squares. The black king on g8 cannot reach the promotion square f8 in time to stop the pawn.

Main line

1.f7! Ke7 2.Kg7! 1-0

Key Idea

When the attacking king controls the promotion path and the defender cannot take opposition, the pawn promotes.

Lesson 2

Black to Move: Opposition Saves the Game

The result changes completely if it is Black to move in Position 1.4. The reason is that the pawn cannot promote if both kings are opposed when the pawn reaches the 7th rank.

Black to move

1...Kg8!

Position 1.5 · Opposition after …Kg8 · White to move

FEN: 6k1/8/5PK1/8/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1

Now the kings are opposed. In this kind of position, the side that has the opposition normally has the advantage, and the side to move is at a disadvantage.

Main line

2.f7+ Kf8! 3.Kf6 stalemate

Other moves do not win against accurate defence. For example:

Variation

2.Kg5 Kf7! 3.Kf5 Kf8! 4.Kf4 Kf7! 5.Kf5 Kf8 6.Ke6 Ke8! 7.f7+ Kf8 8.Kf6 stalemate

Key Idea

If the defending king takes the opposition while the pawn is on the 6th rank, the pawn cannot promote. The major obstacle for the stronger side is often the pawn itself.

Lesson 3

When the Pawn Must Wait

The stronger side must not push the pawn too quickly.

The pawn should only reach the 6th rank when:

  1. its path is clear, or
  2. the advance creates favourable opposition.

Before pushing the pawn, always ask:
Who will have the opposition after the pawn advances?

Lesson 4

Taking the Opposition on the 6th Rank

When the pawn is already on the 6th rank, king manoeuvres driven by opposition decide the result. Taking the opposition first is usually the winning method.

Position 1.6 · Taking the opposition on the 6th rank · White to move

FEN: k7/8/2P5/K7/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1

White to move. The white king on a5 and the black king on a8 are on the same file with two squares between them. White takes the opposition.

Winning move

1.Ka6!

White takes the opposition.

Not 1.Kb6??

1.Kb6?? Kb8!

In that line, Black takes the opposition instead.

Main line

1.Ka6! Kb8 2.Kb6! Kc8 3.c7 Kd7 4.Kb7 and the pawn promotes

Side line: 2…Ka8

If 2…Ka8, Black tries to induce a mistake near the edge.

Correct

3.Kc7!

Not 3.c7??

Then: 3...Ka7 4.Kd7, and the pawn promotes.

Key Idea

When the pawn is on the 6th rank, king manoeuvres are driven by opposition. Taking the opposition first usually decides the result.

Lesson 5

Losing the Opposition

Most mistakes in King + Pawn vs. King endings occur in positions like this. The situation is provisional: the black king cannot keep the opposition because the pawn controls the f7-square.

Position 1.7 · Losing the opposition · White to move

FEN: 8/6k1/4P3/6K1/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1

The best squares for the lone king are normally those in front of the pawn. If the king cannot reach them in the right circumstances, the defence collapses.

Winning move

1.Kf5!

Now, wherever the black king moves, he loses the opposition.

Main line

1...Kg8 2.Kg6! Kf8 3.Kf6 Ke8 4.e7 Kd7 5.Kf7 1-0

Variation

1...Kf8 2.Kf6

Key Idea

Once we take the opposition, the defender loses it if one of the squares he needs is out of reach.

Lesson 6

Knight’s Pawn Warning

Not all pawns work the same. As pawns approach the edge of the board, they often have their own special rules. The rook’s pawn is the most exceptional, but the knight’s pawn also has specific stalemate details because of the nearby edge.

Position 1.8 · Knight’s pawn stalemate resource · White to move

FEN: 8/8/8/8/8/5kp1/7K/8 w - - 0 1

Use this position as a preview or transition to the next lesson. The knight’s pawn on g3 and the nearby edge create stalemate possibilities that do not arise with centre pawns.

Preview Note

Chapter Checklist

  • A pawn on the 6th rank does not automatically win.
  • Opposition decides whether the pawn promotes or the defender holds.
  • The stronger side must not push the pawn too quickly.
  • The defender tries to stay on the two squares in front of the pawn.
  • Taking opposition first is often the winning method.
  • Edge pawns require special care because of stalemate resources.
