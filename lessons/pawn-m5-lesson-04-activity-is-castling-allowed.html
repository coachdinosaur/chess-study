Activity: Is Castling Allowed?

Position 1

White to move. Can White castle kingside?

White: King e1, Rook h1. Black: King e8.

Is castling allowed?

Yes, castling is allowed. The king on e1 has not moved, the rook on h1 has not moved, the king is not in check, squares f1 and g1 are safe, and there are no pieces between the king and the rook. All conditions for kingside castling are met.

2

Position 2

White to move. Can White castle kingside?

White: King e1, Rook h1. Black: Rook e8.

Is castling allowed?

No, castling is not allowed. The White king is in check from the Black rook on e8. The king cannot castle out of check. The check must be dealt with first by moving the king, blocking, or capturing the rook.

3

Position 3

White to move. Can White castle kingside?

White: King e1, Rook h1. Black: Bishop b5, King e8.

Is castling allowed?

No, castling is not allowed. The Black bishop on b5 attacks the f1 square. During castling, the king would pass through f1 on its way to g1. The king cannot cross a square that is under attack. The bishop also attacks along the b5–c4–d3–e2–f1 diagonal.

4

Position 4

White to move. Can White castle kingside?

White: King e1, Knight f1, Rook h1. Black: King e8.

Is castling allowed?

No, castling is not allowed. The White knight on f1 blocks the path between the king on e1 and the rook on h1. For castling to be legal, all squares between the king and the rook must be empty. The knight must move away first.

Castling rules recap: (1) Neither the king nor the rook may have moved. (2) The king must not be in check. (3) The king must not pass through a square under attack. (4) All squares between the king and rook must be empty.
