Capturing a Piece

When a chess piece takes an opponent’s piece, it is called a capture. In notation, a capture is shown with the letter x.

What a capture isLearn that a capture means taking an opponent’s piece.
What x meansUnderstand that x means captures or takes.

What remains after captureThe captured piece is removed and the capturing piece lands on that square.

1

What is a capture?

A capture happens when one piece moves to a square occupied by an opponent’s piece. The opponent’s piece is removed from the board.

Notation rule

In algebraic notation, the letter x means captures or takes.

Piece letter+x+destination square
2

Example: the white knight captures the black bishop

The black bishop is on d3. The white knight moves to d3 and captures it. After the move, the bishop is removed and the knight stands on d3.

1Before the capture

The knight starts on f2. The black bishop is on d3.

2After the capture

The knight lands on d3. The bishop is gone because it was captured.

3

How to read Nxd3

Chess notation is compact. Each part of Nxd3 gives one piece of information.

NKnight
xcaptures
d3landing square

Nxd3 is read as: Knight takes d3.

4

Important idea

The notation Nxd3 does not show the knight’s starting square in this beginner example. It tells us that a knight captured something and finished on d3.

NThe moving piece is a knight.
xThe knight captures an opponent’s piece.
d3The knight finishes on d3.
5

Quick check

Use these review questions to confirm the student understands capture notation.

In Nxd3, what does x mean?

It means the knight captures an opponent’s piece.

After Nxd3, where does the knight stand?

The knight stands on d3.

What happened to the bishop on d3?

The bishop was captured and removed from the board.
