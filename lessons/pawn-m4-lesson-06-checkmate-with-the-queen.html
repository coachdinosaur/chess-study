Checkmate with the Queen

The queen is powerful, so checkmate is easier than with a rook. But you must be careful not to stalemate.

Starting Position

Once you know how to mate with the rook, doing it with the queen is easier. You can still use the shrinking box method.

White to move. White: king h4, queen d2. Black: king f6.

2

Set Up the Box

Qd5 sets up the box and pushes the king toward h8.

Move: 1. Qd5!

3

Continuation

This shrinks the box to 10 squares.

Possible line: 1... Ke7

Another line: 1... Kg6 2. Qe6+ Kg7 3. Kg5 Kf8 4. Qd7!

Main line: 2. Qc6

Both lines: 2... Kd8 3. Qb7!   2... Kf8 3. Qd7!

4

Put the Queen on the Second Row

As soon as the king goes to the side of the board, it is best to place your queen along the second row right away. This avoids the risk of stalemate.

2... Kf7 3. Qd6 Ke8

If: 3... Kg7 4. Qe6

If: 3... Kg8 4. Qe7

Main idea: 4. Qc7!

5

Bring the King In

There is no need to drive the king to the corner, though White could do that with: 5. Qd7 Kg8 6. Qe7

4... Kf8 5. Kg5

Main line: 5... Ke8 6. Kf6 Kf8

6

Four Checkmates

White has four checkmates: 7. Qf7# 7. Qb8# 7. Qc8# 7. Qd8#

Stalemate danger

Sometimes the queen is too powerful. She might stalemate the king if you are not careful.

✓

Review Questions

Use these questions to check understanding.

What method can you use for queen mate?

The shrinking box method.

Why must you be careful with the queen?

The queen controls many squares and can accidentally stalemate the enemy king.

Name one checkmate from the final position.

Qf7#, Qb8#, Qc8#, or Qd8#.
